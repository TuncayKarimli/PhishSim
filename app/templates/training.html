Eight red flags to memorise

If a message shows even one of these, slow down.

Urgency & threats

"Act now or lose access." Pressure is designed to stop you thinking.

🎭

Mismatched sender

A friendly display name over a strange or misspelled email address.

🔗

Suspicious links

Link text says one thing; the real destination is something else.

🔑

Asks for credentials

Real services don't email you to "confirm" your password.

📎

Unexpected attachments

Invoices or files you didn't ask for can carry malware.

👋

Generic greetings

"Dear customer/user" instead of your actual name.

✍️

Spelling & grammar

Odd phrasing and typos in supposedly official mail.

🎁

Too good to be true

Prizes, refunds and gift cards you never entered for.

Know the variants

Phishing comes in many forms

It's not just email. Same trick, different channel.

The classic: mass emails impersonating a trusted brand or service, hoping a fraction of recipients click. Volume over precision.
Targeted at a specific person, using details about your role, colleagues or projects to feel authentic. Much harder to spot.
Spear phishing aimed at executives and finance staff — often a fake "CEO" asking for an urgent wire transfer or gift cards.
Phishing by text message: fake delivery notices, bank alerts or 2FA prompts with a malicious link.
A phone call impersonating IT, your bank or a supplier, pressuring you to read out codes or install "support" software.
A QR code in an email or poster that leads to a fake login page — bypassing link-scanning by hiding the URL in an image.

If something feels off

Four moves that keep you safe

01

Stop

Don't click, download, reply, or scan anything. Take a breath — urgency is the trap.

02

Inspect

Check the real sender address and hover the links. Look for the red flags you just learned.

03

Verify

Confirm any real request through a channel you already trust — not the one in the message.

04

Report

Send it to your security team. One report can protect the whole organisation.
